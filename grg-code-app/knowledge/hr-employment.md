# HR & employment

Reference for HR professionals and employment matters. General guidance, not legal advice — employment law is highly jurisdiction-specific; confirm local rules and have a licensed employment lawyer review dismissals and disputes. Process errors create most of the liability, so get the PROCEDURE right.

## Employment lifecycle
hiring & offer → contract & onboarding → performance management → leave/time → discipline/grievance → change (role/pay) → exit (resignation/dismissal/redundancy).

## Contracts & policies
- **Employment contract essentials**: parties, job title & duties, start date, place of work, hours, pay & benefits, probation, notice period, confidentiality, IP assignment of work product, leave, termination terms.
- **Core policies**: code of conduct, disciplinary & grievance procedure, leave, remote-work, data-protection/acceptable-use, anti-harassment & equal-opportunity.

## Getting procedure right (where liability hides)
- **Discipline/dismissal**: follow a fair process — investigate, put concerns in writing, hold a meeting (right to be accompanied where applicable), allow a response, decide proportionately, give notice, offer appeal. Document every step and date.
- **Redundancy**: genuine business reason, fair selection criteria, consultation, notice/severance per local law.
- **Performance**: set clear expectations → feedback → improvement plan (PIP) with timeline → review.
Always note notice periods, timelines and local statutory minimums to verify.

## Working time, leave, pay
Track working hours, rest breaks, overtime rules, annual/sick/parental leave, minimum wage and payroll obligations — all per local law (verify current figures; don't assume).

## Anti-discrimination
Decisions (hiring, pay, promotion, dismissal) must not be based on protected characteristics. Keep objective, documented, job-related criteria.

## Employee data (GDPR)
Employee records are personal data (some special-category, e.g. health/sick notes).
- Lawful basis is usually **contract** or **legal obligation**, rarely consent (power imbalance).
- Minimise, limit access (HR only), set retention (e.g. delete applicant data after a set period), honour access/erasure requests, and secure the data. Redact names/IDs before using external tools.

## Worked examples
- **Dismissal letter** (structure): reason, the process followed with dates, decision, last day, notice/pay, appeal right.
- **PIP**: specific objectives, support offered, review dates, consequences if unmet.
- **Offer letter / contract clause**: clear, compliant, consistent terms.

## Output style
Give the step-by-step procedure with timelines, draft the letter/policy in a fair and compliant structure, present employer and employee perspectives, flag jurisdiction-specific rules and when a licensed employment lawyer must review before acting, and never invent statutory figures.
