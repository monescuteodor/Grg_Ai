# Contract drafting & review

General reference for legal work, not legal advice. Jurisdiction matters — confirm specifics with a qualified lawyer.

## Anatomy of a contract (review/draft in this order)
1. **Parties** — correct legal names, registration numbers, signatories' authority.
2. **Recitals / background** — context; not operative but aids interpretation.
3. **Definitions** — defined terms Capitalised and used consistently.
4. **Scope / obligations** — who does what, deliverables, acceptance, SLAs.
5. **Price & payment** — amount, currency, VAT, invoicing, due dates, late interest.
6. **Term & termination** — duration, renewal, termination for cause vs for convenience, notice periods, effects of termination.
7. **Confidentiality** — definition, exclusions, duration, permitted disclosures.
8. **IP** — ownership of pre-existing vs created IP, licences, moral rights.
9. **Warranties & representations** — what each side promises is true.
10. **Liability & indemnity** — caps, exclusions (indirect/consequential), carve-outs.
11. **Data protection** — GDPR/DPA where personal data is processed (see the GDPR reference).
12. **Boilerplate** — assignment, force majeure, notices, entire agreement, severability, amendments, waiver.
13. **Governing law & jurisdiction / dispute resolution**.

## Review methodology (how to red-line)
- Read for the **client's role** (buyer vs supplier) — the same clause is "good" or "bad" depending on side.
- Flag **one-sided** terms: uncapped liability, unilateral termination, auto-renewal, broad indemnities, IP assignment of your pre-existing work.
- Check **internal consistency**: defined terms, cross-references, numbering.
- Check **missing** clauses: liability cap, confidentiality duration, data protection, governing law.
- Give a **fallback position** for each risky term (ideal / acceptable / walk-away).
- Note anything that needs a **licensed local lawyer** or jurisdiction-specific check.

## Risk flags (common traps)
- "Indemnify and hold harmless" with **no cap** → add a liability cap.
- **Automatic renewal** with long notice → add an easy opt-out.
- **Unilateral** price changes or termination.
- Confidentiality with **no time limit** or no exclusions (public info, independently developed).
- IP clause assigning **all** IP including pre-existing/background IP.
- Governing law/jurisdiction inconvenient or missing.

## DPA essentials (Art. 28 — when personal data is processed)
Must include: subject-matter & duration; nature & purpose; types of data & data subjects; processor only acts on documented instructions; confidentiality of staff; security measures; sub-processor rules (prior authorisation + flow-down); assistance with data-subject rights & breaches; deletion/return at end; audit rights.

## Example clauses (generic — adapt to the deal)
- **Limitation of liability:** "Except for [confidentiality / IP infringement / death or personal injury], each party's total aggregate liability under this Agreement shall not exceed the fees paid in the 12 months preceding the claim. Neither party is liable for indirect or consequential loss."
- **Confidentiality:** "Each party shall keep the other's Confidential Information secret, use it only to perform this Agreement, and protect it with no less than reasonable care, for the term and 3 years after. This does not apply to information that is public, already known, or independently developed."
- **Termination for convenience:** "Either party may terminate on 30 days' written notice. On termination, the Customer pays for services performed up to the termination date."
- **Governing law:** "This Agreement is governed by the laws of [Romania], and the courts of [Brașov] have exclusive jurisdiction."

## NDA checklist
parties · definition of Confidential Information · exclusions · permitted purpose · term + survival · return/destruction · no licence granted · remedies (injunctive relief) · governing law.

## Output style for legal answers
Be precise and structured. Quote the clause, explain the risk in plain language, give a suggested re-wording, and keep clause numbering. State assumptions and flag where a licensed lawyer or jurisdiction check is required. Never invent statute/case citations — if unsure, say so.
