# Intellectual property

Reference for IP work. General guidance, not legal advice — rights, terms and procedures are jurisdiction-specific. Confirm with a registered IP attorney and do a proper clearance/search before filing. Never invent registration numbers, filing dates or case law.

## The four main rights (what each protects)
- **Trademark** — brand identifiers (name, logo, slogan). Protects against confusingly similar marks in similar goods/services. Registrable; renewable indefinitely (periodic renewals). Do a clearance search first.
- **Patent** — new, inventive, industrially-applicable inventions. Strong but costly; strict novelty (don't publicly disclose before filing); limited term (~20 years). Needs a patent attorney.
- **Copyright** — original works (code, text, design, music). Arises automatically on creation; protects expression, not ideas. Long term. Registration optional in many places but helps enforcement.
- **Trade secret** — confidential business info with value from being secret (e.g. a formula, client list). Protected as long as kept secret → rely on NDAs + access control.
- (Also **registered designs** for the look of a product.)

## Ownership (check this early)
- **Employees**: work created in the course of employment usually belongs to the employer (confirm locally + the contract).
- **Contractors/freelancers**: often keep IP unless the contract **assigns** it in writing — always include an IP assignment clause.
- **Joint/created-with-AI**: clarify in the contract.

## Licensing vs assignment
- **Assignment** = transfer of ownership (permanent). **Licence** = permission to use, owner keeps the right.
- Key licence terms: scope (what), **territory**, **exclusivity** (exclusive/non-exclusive/sole), duration, **royalties/fees**, sublicensing, field of use, termination, moral rights, warranties/indemnity.

## Infringement & enforcement (steps)
1. Confirm you own/validly hold the right. 2. Document the infringement (evidence, dates). 3. Assess similarity/likelihood of confusion (TM) or claim scope (patent) or copying + access (copyright). 4. Usually start with a **cease-and-desist** letter. 5. Negotiate/licence, or escalate to proceedings. Watch limitation periods.

## Filing strategy & deadlines
- Search/clearance before filing (avoid conflicts). 
- **Priority**: an early filing can give a priority date for later filings abroad (tight windows) — verify the exact period.
- Track **renewals** (trademarks, patents) or the right lapses.

## Worked examples
- **IP assignment clause**: "The Contractor hereby assigns to the Client all intellectual-property rights in the deliverables created under this Agreement, and waives moral rights to the extent permitted by law."
- **Trademark check**: before using a brand name, search the register for identical/similar marks in the relevant class(es); flag conflicts.

## Output style
Identify which right applies, give concrete next steps and typical timelines, draft clause-level wording where useful, flag deadlines and the need for a registered attorney/jurisdiction search, and never fabricate registration data or case citations.
