# Litigation: preparing a case for court

General methodology for legal professionals, not legal advice. Procedure, deadlines and remedies are jurisdiction-specific — confirm locally. Never fabricate statutes or case citations; describe the TYPE that applies and tell the user to verify.

## 1. Intake & case theory
- **Parties**: exact legal names, capacity, who can sue/be sued (e.g. an individual vs a company/SRL).
- **Facts**: build a dated **chronology** of what happened, with the document/witness that proves each fact.
- **What the client wants (remedy)**: money (damages), performance, termination, injunction, refund, reinstatement.
- **Limitation / prescription period**: check it FIRST — a missed deadline can kill the case.
- **One-sentence case theory**: "Defendant did X, breaching Y duty, causing the client Z loss; the client is entitled to [remedy]."

## 2. Legal analysis
- Identify the **cause of action** (breach of contract, tort/negligence, consumer protection, unfair dismissal, unpaid invoice, etc.).
- List the **elements to prove** for that cause, and map each element to your evidence (element → evidence → gap).
- Note the **burden and standard of proof** (usually claimant proves, on the balance of probabilities in civil cases).
- Anticipate the **defences** the other side will raise and your rebuttal.

## 3. Evidence
- **Documentary**: contracts, invoices, emails, messages, receipts, photos — organise in a numbered bundle with an index.
- **Witnesses**: who saw what; draft witness statements (identity, what they know first-hand, dated).
- **Expert**: when technical proof is needed (damage, valuation, medical).
- **Preserve & disclose**: don't destroy anything; follow disclosure rules; flag privileged documents.

## 4. Pleadings — statement of claim (typical structure)
1. Court / case heading, parties.
2. Numbered **statement of facts** (one fact per paragraph, chronological).
3. **Legal grounds** (the cause of action and why the facts meet each element).
4. **Remedy sought** (exact sum / order requested) and the **value of the claim** (affects court & fees).
5. **Evidence relied on** and annexes list.
6. Date, signature, required copies.
(The defence/response mirrors this: admit/deny each paragraph + defences + counterclaim if any.)

## 5. Procedure roadmap (generic civil claim)
file claim + pay court fee → service on defendant → defendant's response → (optional mediation/settlement) → evidence/discovery stage → hearings → judgment → **appeal** window → **enforcement** (bailiff/execution) if they don't pay. Track every deadline in a calendar.

## 6. Hearing preparation
- **Skeleton argument**: the issues, your position on each, the evidence and the order you want.
- **Witness questions**: examination-in-chief (open questions) + anticipate cross-examination.
- **Bundle/index** ready, enough copies for court and the other side.
- Practical checklist: documents, ID, power of attorney/authority, timeline, key dates, fallback settlement number.

## Worked example — individual vs a company
Client (consumer/employee) sues a company for e.g. an unpaid refund / unpaid wages / defective product:
- Cause: breach of contract / consumer-protection / labour law.
- Elements: a valid obligation existed → the company failed to perform → the client suffered a quantified loss.
- Evidence: the contract/order, proof of payment, the demand letter and the company's (non-)reply, the loss calculation.
- Pre-litigation step: a **formal demand/notice letter** (often required) giving a deadline to remedy.
- Remedy: the sum owed + interest + costs.
- Draft: demand letter → statement of claim → evidence bundle → hearing skeleton.

## Settlement
Always assess settlement: likely outcome × amount − cost/time/risk. A reasonable early settlement can beat an uncertain judgment. Put any deal in writing.

## Output style
Be structured and practical. Produce usable drafts (demand letter, statement of claim, chronology, evidence list, witness questions). State assumptions and the jurisdiction you assumed, flag deadlines to verify, and mark where a licensed local lawyer must review before filing.
